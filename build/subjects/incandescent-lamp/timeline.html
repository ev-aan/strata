People and bodies involved

  • Humphry Davy: Royal Institution chemist; published the carbon arc in 1812.
  • Frederick de Moleyns: British patent No. 9053, 1841: the earliest incandescent-lamp patent found.
  • John Wellington Starr / Edward Augustin King: British patent No. 10,919, 1845, issued to King as a communication; later sources name Starr as the inventor.
  • de Changy: Belgian; platinum lamps for mines, reported by Jobard in 1858.
  • Alexander Lodygin: Russian; carbon-rod lamps 1872-73; Lomonosov Prize 1874; later metal-filament patents.
  • Henry Woodward and Mathew Evans: Toronto; Canadian patent 3,738 (1874); Woodward's US patent 181,613 (1876).
  • Joseph Swan: Newcastle; carbon lamps shown 1878-79; treated-thread lamp 1880.
  • Thomas Edison and his laboratory: Menlo Park; Charles Batchelor kept the notebook of the Oct 1879 test.
  • William Sawyer and Albon Man: New York; won the Patent Office interference on paper carbons (1885); patent later held invalid.
  • Lewis Latimer: Carbon-manufacturing patent 252,386 (1882), assigned to a rival of Edison's.
  • Heinrich (Henry) Göbel: New York mechanic; claimed 1850s lamps surfaced in 1882 and in 1893 litigation.
  • Judges Wallace, Lacombe, Colt, Hallett, Seaman, Bradley: Wrote the opinions read for this dig.
