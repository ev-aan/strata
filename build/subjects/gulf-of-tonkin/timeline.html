People and bodies involved

  • Lyndon B. Johnson: President. Spoke with McNamara by phone on 3 and 4 August; the calls are on tape (transcribed by Prados).
  • Robert S. McNamara: Secretary of Defense. Said privately on 3 August that OPLAN 34-A 'had bearing on' the Maddox engagement; told the Senate on 6 August the Navy 'was not aware of' South Vietnamese actions (via Prados); in the 4 August call said the ship was 'allegedly' to be attacked that night.
  • McGeorge Bundy: National Security Adviser. At the 5 August 8 a.m. staff meeting said the evidence for the second attack was 'less than we had yesterday', that 'There was an attack', and that he 'welcomed the recent events as justification for a resolution the Administration had wanted for some time'.
  • Douglass Cater: New White House staffer who questioned the need for a resolution at that meeting.
  • William Y. Smith: Military aide; named by Prados as the author of the staff-meeting memorandum (the OCR of the scan does not show the author line).
  • Capt. John Herrick: On-scene commander of the destroyers; his message that contacts 'appear doubtful' is quoted by Prados from a 28 Aug 1964 chronology.
  • Cdr. James Stockdale: Led the Ticonderoga jets overhead on 4 August; later wrote there was 'absolutely no gunfire except our own' (Prados).
  • Adm. Thomas Moorer: Kept a dud shell from a 2 August boat as a souvenir (Prados).
  • Adm. U.S. Grant Sharp: Pacific Fleet commander; proceeded as if the attacks were genuine (tapes commentary).
  • Vo Nguyen Giap: North Vietnam's defense minister; told McNamara in 1995 that nothing happened on 4 August; at a 1997 Hanoi conference said the 2 August action was ordered by a local naval command.
  • Sens. Wayne Morse and Ernest Gruening: The two senators who voted against the Resolution (88-2).
  • J. Norvill Jones and Carl Marcy: Senate Foreign Relations Committee staff who later saw the intercepts; Jones said the 4 August one 'was a boastful summary of the attack on August 2' (Prados).
  • Dr. Louis Tordella: NSA deputy director; in 1972 conceded the 4 August intercept could refer to 2 August and could not produce the original (Hanyok's note).
  • Robert J. Hanyok: NSA historian; wrote the 2001 study, declassified 2005.
  • John Prados: National Security Archive analyst; edited Briefing Book 132 and wrote the 2004 essay used here.
  • NSA units: Phu Bai (Marine detachment, whose analytic error Hanyok says began the 4 August incident), San Miguel (Navy), and the Maddox's own Naval Security Group detachment.
  • North Vietnamese boats: Swatow boats T-142 and T-146 and torpedo boat T-333 appear in the intercepts.
